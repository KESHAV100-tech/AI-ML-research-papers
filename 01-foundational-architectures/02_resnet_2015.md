### 📖 Executive Abstract

> "Deeper neural networks are more difficult to train. We present a residual learning framework to ease the training of networks that are substantially deeper than those used previously. We explicitly reformulate the layers as learning residual functions with reference to the layer inputs, instead of learning unreferenced functions."

---

### 🎯 Core Problem & Motivation

As neural networks grew beyond 20-30 layers, training error worsened due to the 'degradation problem'—not simply vanishing/exploding gradients, but optimization difficulty where deeper networks failed to even learn identity mappings.

---

### 💡 Key Innovations & Architecture

1. **Residual Shortcut Connections**: Formulated stacked layers to learn $F(x) = H(x) - x$ instead of direct mapping $H(x)$, allowing identity pass-through.
2. **Extreme Depth**: Successfully trained networks with 50, 101, and 152 layers (and explored up to 1202 layers on CIFAR).
3. **Bottleneck Block Design**: Used $1 \times 1$, $3 \times 3$, and $1 \times 1$ convolutions to dramatically reduce parameter count and computational complexity while maintaining representational capacity.

---

### 📐 Mathematical Formulation

**Residual Mapping Formulation:**
$$y = \mathcal{F}(x, \{W_i\}) + x$$
Where $x$ and $y$ are input and output vectors, and $\mathcal{F}$ represents the residual function to be learned.

**Gradient Flow Backpropagation:**
$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{E}}{\partial x_l} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{E}}{\partial x_L} \left( 1 + \frac{\partial}{\partial x_l} \sum_{i=l}^{L-1} \mathcal{F}(x_i, W_i) \right)$$
Notice the additive $+1$ term guarantees that gradients flow back directly to early layers without attenuation!

---

### 🌟 Significance & Industry Impact

ResNet achieved a 3.57% top-5 error on ImageNet, surpassing human performance for the first time. Residual connections became an indispensable building block in almost every modern neural architecture, including Transformers.
